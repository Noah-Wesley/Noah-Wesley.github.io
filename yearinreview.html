YEAR IN REVIEW

Over the past year, I personally grew by finally making a few good friends, removing bad habits, implementing good disciplines and work, and reallocating my time to focus on useful, productive things that still bring joy. These were developed from my free time, my work at co-ops, my college ministry, and my strong desire to change.

I am thankful for the friends I made this year and the effort I put into improving myself physically, mentally, and spiritually. However, I regret not asking more questions and retaining information when I do. This was a big problem a year ago, and while it has gotten better, I always am seeking to learn more. I need to soak up information like a sponge and retain it well, and utilize all the resources within reach. My confidence is better, which is good. I just need to listen and work my brain even harder to store that information and truly apply it, so that it doesn't simply travel in and out. While I have seen some improvement in this area already throughout the year, it’s an area where I still need to be striving for more growth.

From my honors self-designed experience this year, I’ve learned that routinely practicing good habits builds character and resilience. The process of enduring challenging and redundant tasks makes us less susceptible to failure, more confident, and more efficient at that same task over time. That lesson exceeds the value of any mere enjoyment brought about by the experience. For my second honors experience, I set realistic, achievable goals of dieting, meal prep, and workout throughout the week. This highly-structured schedule didn't get any easier to follow over time, but rather I adapted to the situation, appreciated the simple things in life much more, and recognized the positive impacts this experience was having that often get underlooked.

Finally, I've learned to create carefully constructed plans for things, and that oftentimes, going in with a plan takes you a lot further in a journey than trying to develop solutions on the fly. For example, in my engineering job, not having a design properly thought-out could cost exponentially more time and money, as design and process errors are realized. This requires patience, but there is beauty in how far patience can take you in life. Of course we still need to be efficient, we shouldn't wait around unnecessarily. We should balance rest and working hard for the best results. Patience isn't just the right way of doing things, it builds character. Slow and steady wins the race.
