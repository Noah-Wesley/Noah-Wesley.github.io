YEAR IN REVIEW

This year, I personally grew by learning a handful of incredibly valuable lessons. These were developed from my courses, my free time, my honors experience, my mistakes, and my personal reflection on all this.

I regret not being as bold this year. I certainly could have asked more questions when I was unsure about topics, utilized more people and more resources at my disposal, and talked with more experts in the fields that interested me such as mechanical engineering, robotics, and entrepreneurship. Looking back to imagine all the opportunities I haven’t heard about because of shyness certainly motivates me to use available resources more often. While I have seen some improvement in this area already throughout the year, it’s an area where I still need to be striving for more growth.

From honors self-designed experience, I’ve learned that sometimes the projects we plan or expect to be highly enjoyable aren’t always enjoyable. Sometimes suffering through parts of the process is necessary. But that is, in many ways, even more valuable than the enjoyment. For my first honors experience, I wanted to research, design and construct a functional electric skateboard, and had very ambitious goals. That plan came with many unforeseen obstacles that got increasingly more difficult until I couldn’t overcome one, but I learned to value the struggle, which motivated me to persevere. Maintaining this attitude of perseverance allowed me to still have fun at times because it made me appreciate the work I was doing. I was fighting to improve things.

Finally, I learned to be more consistent and realistic when setting goals. Going back to my honors experience, it was very ambitious. It’s not a bad thing at all to have an ambitious mindset, but setting massive goals isn’t as realistic as setting incremental, achievable, more conservative goals. These goals make tasks less overwhelming, and they make it easier to see the progress and incremental improvement that is occurring, which is more motivational.
